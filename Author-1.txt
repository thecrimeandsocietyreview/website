Abstract 
The world is currently facing triple planetary crisis: climate change, environmental 
pollution, and biodiversity loss. Biodiversity has important ecosystem functions such as 
carbon absorption, oxygen supply, freshwater, soil fertility, nutrition, and medicine. 
Indonesia has a vital role at the global level because Indonesia has tropical forests with high 
biodiversity, but its sustainability is threatened by wildlife crime. On August 7, 2024, 
Indonesia created a new milestone in biodiversity preservation by enacting Law Number 32 
of  2024 on Amendments to Law Number 5 of  1990 on Conservation of  Natural 
Resources and Ecosystems. It is important to examine the policy breakthroughs in efforts 
to protect biodiversity in Indonesia. This study aims to dissect the legal framework in Law 
Number 32 of  2024 and examine strategic actions to accelerate the eradication of  wildlife 
crime. The study used normative legal methods and content analysis techniques. The 
results show that Law Number 32 of  2024 has 26 new policy breakthroughs, which 
consists of  technical aspects of  conservation, governance, and law enforcement. This 
regulation still has policy gap, namely still emphasizing criminal sanction, the dependence 
of  civil servant investigator on police investigator, fundamental authority that investigators 
do not possess, and the absence of  substances on Forest Ranger. This study also provides 
strategic actions to strengthen law enforcement, namely applying multiple sentences and 
multi-regime investigations, reinforcing science and technology, and strengthening 
collaboration at the global level.

II. Research Problem 
The study shows that Law 5/1990 has weaknesses. First, it does not consider Law Number 
5 of  1960 on Basic Agrarian Principles and the Decree of  the People's Consultative 
Assembly Number IX/MPR/2001 as the basis for regulating natural resources. Second, 
there is a disharmony between Law 5/1990 and other regulations and several 
Constitutional Court verdicts that emphasize the preventive aspect in managing natural 
resources, protection, and respect for the rights of  indigenous peoples and substantive 
public participation. Third, Law 5/1990 is not responsive because does not regulate 
modern biotechnology and genetic resources. Fourth, Law 5/1990 does not provide 
protection for the management and use of  conservation areas by indigenous peoples and 
public participation is only at the informing level. 16
The government's initiative to reform the legal framework was finally realized on 7 August 
2024 with the enactment of  Law Number 32 of  2024 on Amendments to Law 5/1990 
(Law 32/2024). Law 32/2024 amended 18 articles, inserted 9 new articles, and removed 1 
chapter and 1 article, namely regarding the transfer of  affairs and assignment tasks in Law 
5/1990. Although it has been awaited for years, Law 32/2024 has not escaped public 
criticism. In fact, Law 32/2024 was challenged at the Constitutional Court by Aliansi 
Masyarakat Adat Nusantara/AMAN, Walhi, Koalisi Rakyat Untuk Keadilan Perikanan, and a 
farmer named Mikael Ane. The formal review application was registered in case number 
132/PUU-XXII/2024. Law 32/2024 was challenged because it was considered not to 
involve the legal subjects that are closely related to the ecosystem in casu indigenous peoples 
and local communities. 17
As a new policy, the regulatory content of  Law 32/2024 is important to be examined to 
identify policy breakthroughs. This study aims to compare Law 32/2024 with Law 5/1990 
to dissect the latest policy innovation. The emergence of  Law Number 1 of  2023 on the 
Criminal Code (Criminal Code), Law Number 20 of  2025 on the Criminal Procedure Code 
(Criminal Procedure Code), and Law Number 1 of  2026 on Criminal Adjustments (Law on 
Criminal Adjustments) also be the object of  this study.

III. Methodology 
This study is normative juridical that examines positive law aspects. The study attempts to 
analyze laws and regulations that are relevant to the problems, namely Law 5/1990, Law 
32/2024, and other relevant regulations. This study focuses to the norm system. In 
addition to applying the statute approach, a conceptual approach is also utilized to identify 
strategic actions that need to be taken by the government to eradicate wildlife crimes. The 
legal materials collected and analyzed in this study are primary, secondary, and tertiary legal 
materials. The technique used in data analysis is content analysis, which is a method for 
understanding the contents of  a text. The study also uses data on law enforcement 
practices undertaken by Ministry of  Environment and Forestry in 2015 - 2024.

Analysis of  Policy Breakthrough in Law 32/2024 
Law 32/2024 divides three types of  conservation area: Sanctuary Reserve and Nature 
Conservation Area; conservation areas in waters, coastal areas, and small islands; and 
preservation area. Sanctuary Reserve is divided into Strict Nature Reserves and Wildlife 
Sanctuary, while Nature Conservation Area is divided into National Park, Nature 
Recreation Park, and Grand Forest Park. Meanwhile, preservation areas can be in the form 
of  buffer zones for Sanctuary Reserve, Nature Conservation Area, and conservation areas 
in waters, coastal areas, and small islands; ecological corridors or connecting ecosystems; 
areas of  high conservation value; community-managed conservation areas; and/or local 
wisdom protection areas, which can come from protected forest areas, production forests, 
and non-forest area. The government determines the conservation status of  wild plants 
and animals into two categories: protected or unprotected.

Important Notes: The Emergence of  Law Reform in Indonesia 
In 2026, Indonesia marked the emergence of  national legal reform with the enactment of  
three laws: Criminal Code, Criminal Procedure Code, and Law on Criminal Adjustments. 
These three laws came into effect on January 2, 2026. Criminal Code was established to 
replace Wetboek von Strafrecht as stipulated in Law Number 1 of  1946 on Criminal Law 
Regulations which has been amended several times. Criminal Procedure Code was 
established to realize an integrated criminal justice system by adjusting the functions, duties, 
and authorities of  law enforcement officers to align with state developments, advances in 
science and technology, and international conventions that have been ratified by the 
Indonesian Government. The Criminal Adjustment Law then was established as an order 
from the Criminal Code. This law aims to complement the criminal law system to be 
consistent, adaptive, and responsive to social change, so that adjustments are needed to 
criminal law norms that have the potential to cause uncertainty and injustice both between 
laws outside the Criminal Code and Local Regulations with the Criminal Code and within 
the Criminal Code itself. The enactment of  the three laws brings several new implications 
for the governance for handling criminal cases in Indonesia, including:

There are basically two types of  penalties: monetary and non-monetary (which usually 
refers to imprisonment). From a legal perspective, monetary sanctions can either be 
administrative fines or take the form of  criminal fines. Some economists have long 
advocated that when deterrence could be achieved in the same way through those types of  
sanctions, fines would be preferred to imprisonment penalties. The straightforward 
economic reason is that a prison sanction leads to substantial costs for society, whereas 
fines can shift funds from the perpetrator to society.  Then expressionist theorists of  26
punishment often agree on two points: first, that a monetary fine is a more humane and 
efficient punishment than imprisonment and, second, that a monetary fine cannot replace a 
prison sentence, at least for non-minor offenses. 27

be supported by science and technology to strengthen the evidence. In uncovering cases 
related to biodiversity, a science and technology approach is carried out by involving 
experts, forensic support, and digital transformation. These three things are very important 
to accelerate the process of  making accurate decisions. Experts contribute in translating 
scientific evidence into legal evidence. Several scientific fields are needed in handling 
biodiversity cases, including mapping and geospatial, spatial planning, microbiology, 
molecular biology, forensics, wood administration, wildlife utilization, identification of  
species, public policy, psychology, sociology, criminology, intelligence, economic valuation, 
agronomy, and other sciences. 
Forensic support is the application of  scientific knowledge for the purpose of  resolving 
legal problems. The function of  forensics is to provide reliable, accurate, and timely 
information to officers. To uncover wildlife cases, the types of  forensics used are biological 
forensics and digital forensics. Illegal circulation of  wildlife can be traced through 
deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) testing at the wildlife origin and final distribution locations. 
Through biological forensics, the location of  the wildlife habitat can be identified. Digital 
forensics is also needed because today's crimes use communication and information 
technology. Digital forensics is the process of  acquiring, identifying, evaluating, and 
presenting electronic evidence (information extracted from computers or digital devices). 
The science and technology approach can only be realized by involving support from key 
actors, ranging from academics, practitioners, and experts spread across laboratory 
institutions, universities, private institutions, or government agencies. Artificial intelligence 
technology can also be a game changer in eradicating wildlife crimes. Artificial intelligence 
can help officers identify species, verify the contents and documentation of  shipments, 
detect sales via cyberspace, and identify trade patterns. Artificial intelligence thus has great 
potential to reduce the need for personnel and time to conduct field inspections.

in technical aspects of  conservation (3 policy breakthroughs), governance aspects (3 policy 
breakthroughs), and law enforcement aspects (20 policy breakthroughs). Policy 
breakthroughs in the law enforcement aspect were most often found, so there is an 
indication that policy makers place significant expectations on law enforcement to 
strengthen the deterrent effect and behavioural change in society.  The study also identified 
policy gaps in Law 32/2024. It relies heavily on criminal sanctions, makes civil servant 
investigators too dependent on police investigators, does not grant fundamental authorities 
to investigators in building cases, and does not regulate the authority of  forest ranger 
officials. With the enactment of  Criminal Code, Criminal Procedure Code, and Law on 
Criminal Adjustments, some legal breakthroughs in Law 32/2024 are no longer valid, those 
are the reduction of  criminal sanction from maximum 20 year of  imprisonment and a 
Category VIII fine to 15 year of  imprisonment and a Category VII fine, the nullification of  
specific minimum criminal sanction, and the restriction of  civil servant investigator 
authority, especially for arrests and detention. 
The study recommends three strategic actions for the government to accelerate law 
enforcement, namely the application of  layered articles and multi-regime investigations, 
science and technology support, and collaborative networks at the international level.
