Abstract 
The above study is a quantitative analysis of  the influence of  employees’ perception of  salary increase 
policies on the motivational status and productivity of  operations in two major pharmaceutical 
industries in Osun State, Nigeria namely: De-Shalom Pharmaceutical Laboratory Nigeria Limited (Ilesa) 
and Sam-Ace Limited (Ede). The theoretical framework adopted for the study was based on the 
expectancy theory by Victor Vroom (M=E×I×V) and Two-factor theory by Frederick Herzberg. The 
sample used in the study comprises of  n=367 out of  N=4,501 members selected by Taro Yamane 
method of  formula and stratified random sampling in production, quality control, administration, and 
security departments. Quantitative data were gathered through a structured questionnaire with a four-
point Likert scale using SPSS software version 28. Results show that salary is viewed as an important 
hygiene factor, with its absence leading to extreme dissatisfaction and demotivation among workers as 
53.6% of  the respondent’s report lack of  procedural clarity and fairness in current wage adjustment (x 
̅=1.85). Nevertheless, when salary increments are seen as possible rewards tied to performance, there is 
a huge increase in expectations and instrumentality perceptions, leading to increased levels of  work 
effort (80.3%), job dedication (62.9%) and daily task effectiveness (72.7%). The study concludes that 
salary increments should be implemented in a manner that is transparent as motivation rewards based 
on merit and not just maintenance rewards arbitrarily. 
Keywords: Salary Increase, Expectancy Theory, Herzberg Two-Factor Theory, Employee Motivation, 
Operational Productivity, Hygiene Factors, Pharmaceutical Industry. 
1. Introduction 
Organizational management of  employee compensation continues to be one of  the core pillars of  
organizational success, talent retention, and labor relations in the current environment of  developing 
nations (Abbah, 2014; Adeyemi & Ojo, 2024). In the case of  Nigeria, macroeconomic instability, high 
rates of  inflation, and devaluation of  currency have continued to reduce the purchasing power of  the 
populace, thus making salary management an important aspect of  organizational assessment (Adanlawo 
& Nkomo, 2023; Dajo & Akor, 2022). When salary structures do not reflect economic realities or are 
opaque in nature, organizations typically end up having low morale and high labor turnover.

Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Industry in South-Western Nigeria, represented by companies like De-
Shalom Pharmaceutical Laboratory Nigeria Limited (in Ilesa, Osun State) and Sam-Ace Limited 
(Gbongan-Ibadan Road, Ede, Osun State), is characterized by a very strict regulatory framework, which 
involves accuracy, constant monitoring, and adherence to GMP (Good Manufacturing Practices) 
(Alkandi et al., 2023). Adherence to high standards is mostly dependent on motivation, dedication, and 
attention of  people working on factory floors, quality control specialists, and administrative staff  
(Alkandi et al., 2023). However, the process of  wage evaluation in semi-urban manufacturing plants 
tends to be perceived as opaque and inconsistent (Ali, 2020). 
Although many past studies have considered the compensation structure, few empirical studies have 
examined salary increments through the combined perspective of  Vroom's Expectancy Theory and 
Herzberg's Motivation-Hygiene Theory in the Nigerian pharmaceutical manufacturing industry 
(Sowunmi, 2024). The current study fills this void by exploring how pharmaceutical employees appraise 
the process of  salary increments, compute the expectancy-instrumentality drivers and hygiene 
expectations, and the effects of  these expectations on productivity and efficiency of  tasks performed 
each day. 
1.1 Research Questions 
1.What are the perceptions of  employees regarding salary increase practices in De-
Shalom Pharmaceutical Laboratory Nigeria Limited and Sam-Ace Limited? 
2.How does salary increase influence employee motivation through the dual lens 
of  expectancy calculations and motivator-hygiene dynamics? 
3.In what ways do salary increments impact operational productivity and task 
efficiency within the selected pharmaceutical manufacturing firms? 
2. Review Of  Literature & Theoretical Framework 
2.1 Conceptual Review 
Salary Adjustments and Compensation Governance 
The term salary increase can be defined as any increment made on the base monetary pay taking into 
account performance appraisals, experience, cost of  living, or market-based increments (Alananzeh et 
al., 2023; Adeyemi & Ojo, 2024). The pay governance is the system of  regulations, appraisal, and 
communication that guide pay adjustments (John, 2024). In manufacturing environments, a good pay 
governance system makes sure that employees understand the relationship between their performance 
and rewards (Paananen, 2024). 
Workplace Motivation 
Motivation is referred to as the psychological forces that drive and give direction and energy to the 
behavior of  individuals for accomplishing organizational objectives (Ryan & Deci, 2023). Motivation is 
divided into intrinsic factors (getting satisfaction out of  the activity itself) and extrinsic factors 
(involving external monetary and non-monetary rewards) (Zhou & Zhang, 2024). In manufacturing

2.2 Theoretical Framework 
This research incorporates both major motivational process/content theories: Expectancy Theory (V . 
Vroom, 1964) and Two-Factor Theory (F. Herzberg, 1959). 
1. Victor Vroom’s Expectancy Theory 
According to Vroom (1964), the effort a person puts in (M) depends on the product of  three major 
components through the following cognitive calculation: 
 
Expectancy (E→P): Expectancy refers to the subjective probability that the effort (E) put forward by 
the individual will lead to task performance (P). 
Instrumentality (P→R): Instrumentality is the subjective probability that the task performance (P) will 
lead to organizational reward (R) such as salary raise. 
Valence (V): Valence refers to the value that an individual attaches to organizational reward (R). 
If  any one of  the variables is zero, for instance when workers perceive that performance does not 
guarantee increment in salary (I=0) or when workers are paid without considering their performance 
(E=0), motivation will become zero irrespective of  the worth of  the incentive (V). 
2. Frederick Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory 
Herzberg (1959) separated work-related variables into two independent categories: 
•Hygiene Variables (Extrinsic / Dissatisfiers): Such variables as base pay, 
organizational policies, supervision, and physical work environment. Satisfactory 
hygiene variables prevent job dissatisfaction, but do not generate intrinsic job 
satisfaction or motivation. 
•Motivators (Intrinsic / Satisfiers): Such variables as achievement, recognition, 
responsibility, and personal development. They lead to job satisfaction and 
increased effort.

2.3 Integrated Theoretical Model Narrative 
•Hygiene Threshold (Herzberg): Basic salary and the cost-of-living increase 
meet the requirements of  hygiene, ensuring that there is no dissatisfaction and 
administrative frustration at work. 
•Cognitive Process (Vroom): The employee judges the performance appraisal 
process. In case the employee believes in high expectancy (E: "My efforts will 
result in good performance") and high instrumentality (I: "Good performance will 
result in a decent salary raise") along with high valence (V: "Salary raise is 
important to me"), then motivation (M) is greatly increased. 
•Motivator (Herzberg): Performance-based salary raise is acknowledged as an 
explicit recognition of  achievement and importance, thereby turning 
compensation into a motivator. 
•Consequences of  behavior: It is the double process of  psychology that makes 
people work harder and perform better. 
3. Methodology 
3.1 Research Design & Study Area 
The descriptive survey research design was used in this study to obtain quantitative data from two large 
industrial pharmaceutical organizations within Osun State, Nigeria: 
De-Shalom Pharmaceutical Laboratory Nigeria Limited: Peace Haven, Oke Ooye Street, Iyemogun, 
Ilesa, Osun State (specialized in compounding, oral liquids, and tablets production). 
Sam-Ace Limited: Km 14 Gbongan-Ibadan Road, Ede, Osun State (specialized in OTC pain relievers, 
ORS, and liquids). 
3.2 Target Population and Sample Size 
The target population size (N): N=4,501 employees from both companies (2,201 for De-Shalom and 
2,300 for Sam-Ace) including Production, Quality Control, Human Resources/ Administration, and 
Security/Maintenance.

2023; Sowunmi, 2024). 
6. Conclusion & Policy Recommendations 
6.1 Conclusion 
This study investigates how the employees perceive the process of  salary increase, workplace 
motivation, and organizational productivity at De-Shalom Pharmaceutical Laboratory Nigeria Limited 
and Sam-Ace Limited based in Osun State, Nigeria. Drawing from the framework of  the Expectancy 
Theory by Vroom and Two Factor Theory by Herzberg, it can be seen that although the existing 
process of  wage review is considered opaque and inconsistent, salary is still a crucial motivating factor 
for the employees’ work and productivity. The process of  transparent salary payment is a source of  
hygiene factors and motivates the employees. 
6.2 Policy Recommendations 
•Enhancing Instrumentality by Ensuring Good Pay Governance: The 
management should define and communicate performance evaluation standards 
objectively, which have a direct relation between performance evaluations and 
salary increases. 
•Implementing Pay Review Cycles: A consistent cycle of  annual or biannual 
reviews should be put into place to ensure that wages keep up with inflation, 
retaining the role of  salary as a good hygiene factor. 
•Improving Appraisal Communication: HR managers should give structured 
feedback on performance evaluations to increase expectancy (E →P) of  
employees.
